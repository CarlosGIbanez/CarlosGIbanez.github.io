We can define _planning as the pre-selection of actions in order to achieve desired outcomes_.

So, how can you make a good one? The short answer is: identify your goal, make a plan, figure it out why it will fail, patch it, then repeat.

These ideas are heavily inspired by [Neel Nanda's blogs](https://dynalist.io/d/pgngbu6nDQbQzu1Gcof3Dj1i), so definitely check them out!

We can synthesize the process of making an effective plan as:

1. Identythe problem you want to solve (or the goal you want to achieve)
2. Make a plan
3. Identify possible failure modes
4. Patch it
5. Repeat
6. Do it!

## 1. Identify the goal

What exactly is the thing you want to acomplish or the problem you want to solve? The more specific the description, the better.

Also, I recommend exploring the problem/goal first to see if it's truly what you want to do/solve. For goals you can do goal-chaining: Identify your [terminal goals](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/terminal-versus-instrumental-goals-values-preferences) and chain down the things you need to do (your instrumental goals) in order to achieve them; see if your goal is truly in that set (if it will truly bring you closer to your terminal goals). For problems, identify if it's a blocker, a bottleneck or something else; what is it stopping you from achieving? If the problem disappears, will you really be closer to your goals?

## 2. Make a plan

As detailed and concrete as you can. I recommend doing checklists and sub-goals.

E.g., say I want to learn calculus. Then I need to:

- Learn the intuition behind limits
  - Practice exercises
  - Do flashcards
- Learn fundamentals of the derivative
  - Compress my knowledge
  - Practice exercises
  - Do flashcards
- Learn the fundamentals of integrals
  - ...

Also identify necessary resources, people, and other kinds of enablers.

## 3. Identify possible failure modes

Go through your plan and ask: How can I fail at this? Would I be surprised if I did?

Imagine if, at the end, you actually fail to meet your final goal. Why is that? Generate reasons and score them by how surprised you'd be if it happened

Continuing with the calculus example:

- I fail to build the habit of practicing my flashcards (not surprised at all)
- I fail to do my exercises (a little surprised)

Actually try to mentally walk through your plan, feel each step viscerally, and identify friction points where you might fail. Then see if, once every step has been completed successfully, something still feels missing.

## 4. Patch your plan

Once you identify problems in your plan, come up with solutions.

Following the example:

- Build the habit of practicing my flashcards at 10 am every day
- Learn the intuition behind limits
  - Practice exercises
    - Schedule: Thursday 3 pm
  - Do flashcards
- Learn fundamentals of the derivative
  - Compress my knowledge
  - Practice exercises
    - Schedule: Friday 11 am
  - Do flashcards
- Fundamentals of integrals
  - ...

## 5. Repeat

Repeat steps 2-4 until you feel you have a solid plan.

Note: Unexpected things will inevitably come up, so you need the flexibility to adapt your plan on the go.

## 6. Actually start doing it

It's easy to just stay in the planning phase and never actually act on it.

Start with the first step of your plan immediately.
